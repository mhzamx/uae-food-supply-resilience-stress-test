# UAE Food Supply Resilience Stress Test

**Executive summary · Frozen analytical baseline · 6 October 2026**  
**Project lead: Miguel Ángel Hernández Aranda**  
UAE Data Impact Project 2026, Project 2

## What the evidence supports

Alternative routes provide substantial physical headroom for the UAE’s priority foods, but public data cannot establish a single observed national food-coverage percentage. This stress test compares two scopes in parallel: **C1-only (UAE east-coast ports)** and the **regional envelope (C1 + Sohar + Duqm + Salalah)**. C1 is the UAE-attributable floor of the port analysis; the regional envelope is a conditional ceiling that includes Oman’s own demand and transshipment. Neither scope is designated the primary result.

Under the reference allocation benchmark, C1-only Low activity supports approximately **99.3%–91.8% of the modeled port-routed shipment requirement**, across low–high resident demand. The modeled gap is **44.9–621.3 tonnes per day**. The regional envelope supports **100%** of that port-routed requirement at Low activity if its flows can be accessed and transferred to the UAE. The physical ceiling is 100% in both scopes. These are scenario results, not measured food deliveries or evidence of an observed shortage. [Brecha J/O; Executive summary; S24, S81–S82]

The C1 container case is **borderline and allocation-sensitive**. Low includes the initial ramp-up; the benchmark has confidence C. Counting total C1 activity, including its baseline, reduces the high-demand gap to **404.9 t/day** and closes the low-demand gap. Dry bulk does not bind under the benchmark. The practical uncertainty is how port activity becomes usable food supply through allocation, inland road and rail transfer, customs and cold-chain execution. Supplier concentration and route exposure remain separate risks even when modeled port coverage reaches 100%. [Brecha Q; Findings F01–F04; E32, E36–E38]

## Scope and results

The basket comprises the nine essential **price-policy categories**: cooking oil, eggs, dairy, rice, sugar, poultry, pulses, bread and wheat. It differs from the 18-item National Food Security Strategy 2051 basket. Bread is an HS190590 exposure proxy and receives no separate requirement or gap because it overlaps with Wheat and products. [S25–S27; Results]

Demand is the **estimated 2026 resident import-dependent requirement**, derived from 2023 FAOSTAT balances and resident scaling. Tourism remains a separate sensitivity. Requirements are converted to physical shipment weight with commodity-specific Comtrade/FAOSTAT factors before comparison with cargo activity; equivalent milk, wheat or raw-sugar units are not added directly to physical shipments. [Requerimiento neto; S56, S72–S73, S81]

|---|---:|---:|---|
| Containers + dry bulk | 6,701.165 t/day | 7,540.622 t/day | About 95.7% of physical shipment requirement; modeled port segments |
| Fresh poultry + fresh dairy, TER | 176.492 t/day | 202.126 t/day | Regional road-route requirement; mode and effective flow unverified |

TER and GL together account for about 4.3% of physical shipment requirement. They are reported separately and are not treated as either zero coverage or observed gaps. [Brecha J/L/N]

The reference benchmark allocates **19.6656%** of incremental total cargo to the analytical food requirement, based on broad HS01–24 weight relative to UAE non-fuel imports in 2023. It does not observe food’s share of incremental cargo or its composition by port segment. At C1-only Low, container coverage is 98.99%/87.65%; dry-bulk coverage is 100% in both demand cases. C1-only Base and High synchronized close the benchmark port gap, as do all three regional activity levels. [Brecha G/J/O/P; S82]

The high-demand container threshold is **25,577.5 t/day of total incremental cargo** under that benchmark. C1’s first eight closure weeks averaged approximately 14,852 t/day; the remaining 21 weeks averaged 25,300 t/day, close to the threshold. The synchronized C1 High is **34,673.7 t/day**, using its own four-week maximum, with week starts 6–27 July. It must not be substituted with C1’s value during the regional maximum window. [Brecha O/Q; corridor script v0.2]

## Commodity priorities

Priorities combine four analytical judgments: modeled port stress, inland/cold-chain uncertainty, supplier concentration and inferred route exposure. They are not a computed risk score. [Results]

| Priority | Evidence and implication |
|---|---|
| Frozen poultry | Brazil supplies 83.2% by import value; the C1-only Low high-demand gap is 166.9 t/day, with reefer dependence. Diversification and cold-chain execution deserve attention together. |
| Rice | India supplies 72.1% by value; the corresponding container gap is 160.5 t/day. Pakistan and Viet Nam are observed alternative origins; their scalable substitution is not established. |
| Raw sugar | Brazil supplies 98.7% by value despite no modeled dry-bulk gap. Port headroom does not remove origin concentration. |
| Fresh dairy and fresh poultry | Regional origin is compatible with road supply, but does not prove road flow. The optimistic origin-based TER residual is about 49.8–57.2 t/day; central regional credit remains zero. |
| Crude/upstream cooking oil and wheat | Confirmed inferred Red Sea exposure within the Top Five suppliers is 47.7% and 56.6%, respectively. Cape-compatible sourcing and route fallbacks matter independently of port coverage. |

Concentration and route shares are **by import value**. The TER residual instead uses **weight shares within each subgroup**: Saudi Arabia + Oman account for 95.63% of fresh poultry and 65.06% of fresh/yogurt dairy weight. Routes are inferred by origin country, not tracked by shipment; Türkiye, Canada and US cases retain their conditional classifications. [S54, S75, S77, S81, S83; Brecha R; Exposición de ruta]

The analytical basket’s import-dependent nutritional contribution is **28,771.7–32,254.1 million kcal/day** and **763.6–860.6 t of protein/day**, calculated with exact RNI A values. Under high demand, wheat contributes about 33.4% of modeled energy and 39.4% of protein; cooking oils contribute 27.4% of energy, poultry 27.6% of protein and pulses **13.76%** of protein. These shares describe this basket, not the whole UAE food system. Pulses retain lower confidence because of the large FAOSTAT residual. [Nutrición; S72, S84; correction after E38]

## Actions supported by the analysis

1. Report both scopes, physical ceilings and allocation benchmarks together. Use the 25,577.5 t/day threshold as a transparent scenario trigger, not a guarantee of food availability.
2. Measure UAE-usable access to Oman flows, border turnaround, inland transfer and food/reefer eligibility. Closing the high-demand C1 Low gap would require about 3,159.5 t/day of cargo-equivalent under the same benchmark, or 47.22% of the C2b Low increment.
3. Prioritize origin diversification for raw sugar, frozen poultry, rice and eggs, while screening scale, sanitary access, price and route compatibility before treating alternatives as substitutes.
4. Preserve separate TER/GL status fields, nutrient contributions and confidence labels. Revisit the result when food-specific allocation or inland throughput is observed.

Official publications establish a road-and-rail response within C1, including bonded customs corridors and a dedicated Fujairah–ICAD service. Reefer capability is also documented. They do not identify available food-specific daily throughput; the model therefore does not turn these capabilities into guaranteed coverage. It credits no operational cross-border rail flow from Oman in this frozen version. [Recommendations; S85–S88]

## Evidence and reproducibility

The calculation uses observed AIS-based **total cargo activity**, inferred route exposure and explicit demand/allocation scenarios. C3a remains a separate sensitivity because of the King Abdullah Port PortWatch anomaly. Synchronization prevents adding peaks from different weeks within a segment; segment maximum windows are not assumed to coincide. PortWatch activity is not nominal or available port capacity. [S24, S36–S47; Brecha I]

The final analytical text preserves the approved central gap calculation and incorporates the exact-RNI nutrition correction. Source QC reviewed S01–S89 and tested 99 distinct registered URLs: some returned only dynamic interfaces or retrieval failures. The QC register records these limits; it does not claim every original data extraction was independently rerun. Publication packaging, data-reuse terms and executable dashboard validation remain distinct release checks.
